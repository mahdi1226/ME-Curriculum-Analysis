## KEY OBSERVATIONS

### Strengths (vs. UMSL):
1. **Dedicated Vibrations course** (MEMS 4310) + Vibrations Lab (MEMS 4050) — UMSL has NONE of this (ABET gap)
2. **Machine Shop Practicum** (MEMS 1100) — hands-on early exposure
3. **Three separate soft-skills courses** (Ethics, Leadership, Conflict Management) — ENGR 4501/4502/4503
4. **Design of Thermal Systems** (MEMS 3430) — dedicated thermal design course beyond core
5. **Solid Mechanics** (MEMS 3530) — advanced course beyond basic mechanics of materials
6. **Modeling, Simulation and Control** (MEMS 4320) — integrates M/S/C concepts
7. **Numerical Methods and Matrix Algebra** (MEMS 2210) — dedicated numerical methods course
8. **Three lab courses** — MEMS 2050, 3050, 4050 (Mechanics/Materials, Fluids/HT, Vibrations)

### Structural Notes:
- **Combines Statics and Mechanics of Materials** into one course (MEMS 2510) — UMSL separates them (ENGR 2310 + ENGR 2332)
- **Lower total credits (120 vs 125 at UMSL, 128-129 at S&T/SIUE/SLU)** — WashU has the leanest ME program
- **Starts Calculus at II, not I** — assumes AP Calc AB/BC placement or prior Calc I credit
- **Only 3 courses in Senior Electives** — similar to SLU's 4, peers have flexibility

### WashU MEMS = Materials Science Integration
The department is "MEMS" (Mechanical Engineering and Materials Science) — integrated department. Materials Science (MEMS 2610) is taught by ME department, not separated like at UMSL (MENG 3340 is separate).

### Relationship to UMSL
Historically, UMSL students went to WashU via the Joint Engineering Program (ending 2026). WashU is the aspirational benchmark — as a new school, UMSL is positioning itself differently (public, urban, commuter).

## Interview Talking Points

### When they ask: "Why UMSL?"
> "UMSL's School of Engineering is building three programs from scratch. My research on Course Prerequisite Networks is specifically designed to optimize this kind of curricular structure. I've published a peer-reviewed framework for analyzing prerequisite bottlenecks, predicting path completion probability, and identifying grade transition patterns. I can apply these methods directly to UMSL's new programs — not after the curriculum is fixed, but during the design phase, when the impact is greatest."

### When they ask: "What courses can you teach?"
> "My core strength is the mathematical foundations of ME — differential equations, system dynamics, controls, probability and statistics. I can also teach computing with an engineering focus using Python, which is increasingly expected in modern ME curricula. But beyond individual courses, I bring the ability to analyze how courses connect to each other as a system. My CPN research shows that it's not enough to design good individual courses — you need to optimize the prerequisite structure between them."

### When they ask: "How would you contribute to ABET accreditation?"
> "I've identified three gaps in the current UMSL ME curriculum relative to ABET's ME program criteria: mechanisms, vibrations, and technical elective flexibility. Beyond that, my research provides a framework for ABET Criterion 4 — Continuous Improvement. I can build a data-driven assessment system using graph-based analytics that tracks student progression through prerequisite chains, identifies high-risk bottleneck courses, and quantifies the probability that students complete the program on time. This goes far beyond standard ABET assessment practices."

### When they ask: "What's your vision for the program?"
> "Three things. First, fix the ABET gaps — add mechanisms and vibrations, create elective flexibility. Second, integrate AI and modern computing throughout the curriculum, not as add-ons but woven into existing courses. Third — and this is what makes me different from most NTT candidates — use data-driven curriculum analytics to continuously optimize the program structure. As a new program, UMSL has a rare opportunity to do this right from the start, rather than inheriting decades of legacy prerequisite chains that resist change."

### When they ask about diversity/inclusion:
> "My CPN research is directly motivated by student retention. Our published work shows that ME has the longest critical paths and the lowest path completion probability of any major we studied. The DEEP project demonstrated that these long prerequisite chains disproportionately harm under-represented students who are less likely to have been steered toward engineering from high school. By analyzing and shortening critical paths, we don't just improve efficiency — we make the program more accessible to students who arrive with different preparation levels."

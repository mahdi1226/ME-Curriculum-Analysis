ABET Overview

ABET's Engineering Accreditation Commission (EAC) evaluates post-secondary engineering programs against a set of quality standards to ensure that graduates are prepared for professional practice. Accreditation is essential for licensure eligibility (PE/FE exams), employer confidence, and graduate school admissions.

EAC accreditation evaluates programs on 8 General Criteria:

  1. Students
  2. Program Educational Objectives
  3. Student Outcomes
  4. Continuous Improvement
  5. Curriculum
  6. Faculty
  7. Facilities
  8. Institutional Support

In addition, the ME-specific Program Criteria require demonstrated coverage of thermal, mechanical, and mixed systems with appropriate depth in mathematics, physical sciences, and engineering topics.

UMSL Status: The UMSL Mechanical Engineering program launched in 2025 and is a new program seeking initial ABET accreditation. The first graduating class is expected around 2029, at which point the program can formally apply for an ABET evaluation visit.

Gap Analysis

The following gaps were identified through comparison with ABET ME Program Criteria and peer program benchmarking. Each gap poses a risk to successful accreditation.

Gap 1: Mechanisms / Kinematics

What It Is

Kinematics and dynamics of mechanisms covers the analysis and synthesis of linkages, cams, gears, and other mechanical devices that convert and transmit motion. This includes position, velocity, and acceleration analysis of multi-body systems.

Why ABET Needs It

The ME Program Criteria explicitly require coverage of mechanical systems including mechanisms. ABET evaluators will look for a course or clear curricular thread addressing mechanism design and analysis.

What Peers Have

Nearly all ABET-accredited ME programs include a dedicated Mechanisms or Kinematics course (typically 3 credits, junior level). Examples: Missouri S&T (ME 2511), Mizzou (MAE 3600), SLU (AENG 3300).

Recommendation

Add a new course MENG 33XX – Kinematics & Dynamics of Mechanisms (3 credits) as a required junior-level course, with ENGR 2320 (Dynamics) as prerequisite.

Gap 2: Vibrations

What It Is

Mechanical vibrations covers free and forced vibration of single- and multi-degree-of-freedom systems, modal analysis, damping, resonance, and vibration isolation/control.

Why ABET Needs It

Vibrations is a core ME topic under mechanical systems and appears in virtually every accredited ME curriculum. Its absence would be noted during an ABET visit.

What Peers Have

Peer institutions offer standalone vibrations courses (typically 3 credits): Missouri S&T (ME 3521), Mizzou (MAE 3700), SLU (AENG 3400).

Recommendation

Option A (preferred): Create a standalone course MENG 33XX – Mechanical Vibrations (3 credits).
Option B: Integrate vibrations content into MENG 3350 (Control Systems), expanding it to include vibration fundamentals before controls topics. This option is less ideal as it compresses two distinct subjects.

Gap 3: No Technical Electives

What It Is

The current curriculum prescribes all upper-division courses with no elective choices for students. Every student takes the exact same set of MENG 3000/4000-level courses.

Why ABET Needs It

ABET values curricula that allow students to develop individual competencies and pursue areas of interest. Elective flexibility also supports Criterion 1 (Students) by accommodating diverse student goals.

What Peers Have

Peer programs typically offer 9–12 credit hours of technical electives from a curated menu of advanced ME topics (robotics, HVAC, composites, biomechanics, automotive engineering, etc.).

Recommendation

Create an elective menu of 6–8 upper-division MENG courses and require students to complete 9 credits of technical electives. Convert some existing required courses (e.g., MENG 4450, MENG 4490, MENG 4460) into elective options and develop new elective offerings.

Gap 4: Prerequisite Grade Requirements

What It Is

The program does not explicitly state minimum grade requirements for prerequisite courses. Students can theoretically advance through the curriculum with D grades in foundational courses.

Why ABET Needs It

ABET Criterion 1 (Students) and Criterion 5 (Curriculum) expect programs to have clear policies ensuring that students have adequate preparation before advancing to upper-level courses. Lack of grade gates raises concerns about student readiness.

Recommendation

Implement a policy requiring a grade of C or better in all gateway courses (Calculus sequence, Physics sequence, Statics, Dynamics, Thermodynamics, Mechanics of Materials) before students may enroll in downstream courses.

ABET Timeline

Projected timeline from program launch to initial accreditation decision.

2025 — Program Launch

UMSL Mechanical Engineering program admits its first cohort. Curriculum based on 128 credit-hour plan covering math, science, engineering fundamentals, ME core, and general education.

2026–2027 — Curriculum Refinement

Address identified gaps: add Mechanisms and Vibrations courses, establish technical elective menu, implement prerequisite grade policies, and update ethics content. Begin collecting assessment data for Student Outcomes.

2028 — Self-Study Preparation

Begin preparing the ABET Self-Study Report. Compile assessment data, document continuous improvement actions, organize faculty credentials, and prepare facility documentation. Conduct an internal mock review.

~2029 — First Graduates & ABET Request

First cohort graduates. Submit Request for Evaluation (RFE) to ABET. The program must have graduates (or students near graduation) before an evaluation visit can be scheduled.

2029–2030 — ABET Evaluation Visit

ABET Program Evaluator(s) conduct an on-site visit. Review curriculum, facilities, faculty, student work, assessment processes, and continuous improvement evidence. Respond to any findings in the Draft Statement.

~2030 — Initial Accreditation Decision

ABET Engineering Accreditation Commission issues its accreditation action. If granted, accreditation is retroactive to the last graduation date before the visit, covering the first graduating class.

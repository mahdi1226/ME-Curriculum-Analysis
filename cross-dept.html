Overview

UMSL's School of Engineering offers three undergraduate programs: Mechanical Engineering (ME), Electrical Engineering (EE), and Civil Engineering (CE). As a newly established school (2025), these programs share a substantial common foundation, which is typical of ABET-accredited engineering curricula.

Understanding this shared structure is important for:

  • Resource efficiency -- one section of a shared course serves students from multiple programs
  • Faculty planning -- identifying who teaches shared vs. department-specific courses
  • Student flexibility -- students who switch majors in Year 1 or Year 2 lose minimal progress
  • Scheduling -- shared courses require more sections due to higher enrollment across programs

Resource Implications

Faculty Needs

  • Shared courses (Math, Physics, Chemistry, English): Taught by existing UMSL departments -- no new engineering faculty needed.
  • Shared engineering courses (Statics, Dynamics, Mechanics of Materials): Need 2-3 engineering faculty who can teach across ME and CE.
  • ME-specific courses: Need 4-5 ME faculty covering thermal sciences, mechanical design, controls, manufacturing, and materials.
  • AI and modern topics: Need 1-2 faculty with computational and AI expertise for emerging course content.

Scheduling Considerations

  • Shared courses need multiple sections since they serve students from all 3 programs simultaneously.
  • Year 1-2 courses (calculus, physics, statics) have the highest enrollment and need the most sections.
  • ME-specific courses (Year 3-4) have lower enrollment and can typically run as single sections.
  • Careful scheduling avoids conflicts between required courses in the same semester.

Student Flexibility

  • ME to CE (or CE to ME): Students can switch up to the end of Year 2 with minimal course loss, since 53 + 9 = 62 shared credits overlap.
  • ME to EE (or EE to ME): Students can switch up to the end of Year 1 with minimal loss, since 53 + 3 = 56 shared credits overlap but mechanics courses diverge in Year 2.
  • After Year 2 Spring: Switching departments requires significant additional coursework because department-specific branches have diverged.

Bottleneck Courses

  • MATH 1800 (Calculus I): Root prerequisite for nearly every engineering course. Delays here cascade through the entire curriculum.
  • ENGR 2310 (Statics): Gates Dynamics, Mechanics of Materials, and all downstream ME and CE courses.
  • MATH 2020 (Diff. Equations): Required for Fluid Mechanics, System Dynamics, and Heat Transfer simultaneously.
  • EENG 2310 (Circuit Analysis): Gates Instrumentation and Applied Thermo for ME students.

Comparison with SIUE

Both UMSL and SIUE serve ME, EE, and CE programs with a large shared foundation. However, the two schools take different approaches to naming and organizing shared courses.

Aspect UMSL SIUE
Shared course naming Uses ENGR-prefix for shared courses (e.g., ENGR 2310 = Statics) Uses cross-listed prefixes (e.g., CE/ME 240 = Statics)
Ownership signal ENGR prefix signals shared ownership -- no single department "owns" the course Cross-listing signals joint ownership between named departments
Mechanics shared courses ENGR 2310 (Statics), ENGR 2320 (Dynamics), ENGR 2332 (Mech. of Materials) CE/ME 240 (Statics), CE/ME 242 (Mech. of Solids), ME 262 (Dynamics)
Circuits shared course EENG 2310 (Circuit Analysis I) -- ME + EE ECE/ME 210 (Circuits) -- ME + ECE
Total shared credits ~53 credits shared by all 3 programs ~50+ credits shared by all 3 programs
Flexibility advantage ENGR prefix is more flexible -- easier to add a 4th program without renaming Cross-listing is explicit but requires renaming if sharing changes
Math root course MATH 1800 (Calculus I, 5 credits) MATH 150 (Calculus I, 4 credits)
Critical bottleneck ENGR 2310 (Statics) gates ME + CE branches CE/ME 240 (Statics) and CE/ME 242 (Mech. of Solids) gate ME + CE branches

Key Takeaway

Both UMSL and SIUE share a similar structural pattern: a deep math/physics foundation feeds into shared engineering mechanics courses, which then branch into department-specific sequences. UMSL's use of the generic ENGR prefix for shared courses is a more modern and scalable approach compared to SIUE's cross-listed naming convention. Both schools have approximately 50+ shared credits, meaning roughly 40% of any engineering degree consists of courses common to all three programs.
